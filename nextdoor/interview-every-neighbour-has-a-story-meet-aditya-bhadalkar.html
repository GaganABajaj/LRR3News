Interview: Every Neighbour Has a Story – Meet Aditya Bhadalkar

By Community Desk  ·  07 Nov 2026 · 23:34

Even small contributions, when made collectively, can bring about meaningful improvements.

An easy-going Mumbaikar, an IT professional by occupation, a traveller by passion, and someone who values his peace of mind above almost everything else!

Part 1: A little introduction

What is your full name, and what do you like to be called?

My full name is Aditya Bhadalkar. Most people who know me call me Aditya, and I'm perfectly happy keeping it that way. No fancy nicknames required!

Which flat do you live in, and how long have you been part of our society?

I am a resident of the E Wing of R3 Society. It's been a part of my residential journey, with its own share of experiences, observations and stories along the way.

Who all are there in your family?

It's a small family of three – my better half, our son and me. A little team that keeps life interesting, entertaining and occasionally unpredictable!

Where are you originally from?

I am originally from Mumbai. Having grown up in a city that rarely slows down, I suppose a little bit of that fast-paced Mumbai spirit has stayed with me.

How would you describe yourself in three words?

Serious, friendly and moody. Quite an interesting combination, I know! I can be serious when the situation demands, friendly when the company is good, and moody when my mind decides to have a personality of its own.

Part 2: Your journey

What brought you to this city or LR?

A combination of professional opportunities and parental expectations brought me here. Like many life decisions, it was a mix of practical considerations, family priorities and circumstances that eventually shaped the journey.

What do you do professionally? Tell us a little about your work.

I work in the IT industry – an industry that is often overly glorified but, in reality, comes with its own share of challenges, uncertainties and occasional surprises!

My work revolves around cloud infrastructure, product implementation and project management. I also work closely with global clients, which means coordinating across teams, managing expectations, solving problems and ensuring things move in the right direction.

In simple terms, I spend a good part of my professional life making sure technology works, projects move forward and people remain reasonably happy in the process!

What has been the most exciting or memorable chapter of your life so far?

One of the most memorable chapters was navigating my first job outside Maharashtra. Moving away from familiar surroundings, adjusting to a new place and finding my feet professionally wasn't exactly a cakewalk.

However, that phase also gave me some wonderful friendships with colleagues who gradually became much more than just people I worked with. The struggles, the laughter, the disagreements, the experiences and all those bittersweet moments made it a chapter worth remembering.

Sometimes, it's not the place or the job that makes a journey memorable, but the people you meet along the way.

Part 3: Beyond work – the person behind the profession

What do you enjoy doing in your free time?

I enjoy reading and binge-watching. Depending on my mood, I could spend hours immersed in a good read or completely hooked on a series. Sometimes, a little screen time and absolutely no responsibilities make for a perfectly good break!

Do you have any hobbies or hidden talents?

Table tennis is one of my favourite hobbies. I genuinely enjoy the game, whether it's a casual rally or a competitive match. It's a great way to stay active, have fun and occasionally discover how competitive you can get over a small ball!

Are you passionate about cooking, gardening, travelling, music, reading, sports, art, or something else?

Travelling would definitely be right at the top of my list. I enjoy exploring new places, experiencing different surroundings and, more than anything, the journey itself. For me, travelling is as much about the experiences along the way as it is about reaching the destination.

What does your ideal weekend look like?

I'm an early riser, so my ideal weekend starts early. I like making the most of the day rather than waking up to discover that half the weekend has already disappeared!

Ideally, it would involve a road trip, some exploration and good company. But I'm equally happy spending the day at home, doing absolutely nothing and having no particular agenda. Sometimes, the best weekend plan is having no plan at all!

What is one thing you have always wanted to learn or try but haven't got around to yet?

Cooking! It's something I've wanted to explore for a while but haven't quite managed to get around to. Considering how much we depend on good food, learning to prepare something beyond the basics would definitely be a useful life skill. Who knows, perhaps there's a chef waiting to be discovered!

Part 4: The personal favourites

Your favourite holiday destination?

I'm more of a 'love the journey than the destination' kind of person. I don't have one particular favourite destination. For me, the excitement lies in hitting the road, discovering new places and enjoying the experiences along the way.

Wherever the road takes me is good enough, provided the journey is worth remembering!

Your favourite food or comfort meal?

I'm not particularly a foodie. My food preferences are fairly uncomplicated – whatever satisfies my hunger at that moment works for me. I'm probably one of the easier people to decide a restaurant for!

Mountains or beaches?

Beaches, without a doubt! There's something about the sea, the open horizon and the sound of waves that makes it easier to disconnect from everything else.

Early bird or night owl?

Definitely an early bird. I like starting my day early and making the most of the time available. There's something satisfying about getting a head start while the rest of the world is still waking up.

One person, living or historical, you would love to have dinner with?

My maternal grandmother, probably. There are some conversations you wish you could have again, and some people whose presence you realise you can never quite replace. A dinner with her would be special, regardless of what was on the menu.

What is your idea of a perfect day?

Any day that doesn't disturb my peace of mind qualifies as a perfect day! No unnecessary drama, no avoidable stress and no unexpected complications. If the day goes by peacefully, I'm more than happy.

Part 5: Life in our society

What made you choose this society as your home?

There wasn't any particularly elaborate thought process behind the decision. I liked what was on offer at the time, it seemed to meet my requirements, and the decision eventually worked out as a practical choice.

What was your first impression when you moved in?

Honestly? Regret! That's probably not the answer people expect, but I'd rather be honest than manufacture a diplomatic response. First impressions aren't always the final impressions, though, and life has a way of adding different perspectives over time.

What do you enjoy most about living here?

The neighbours. At the end of the day, a residential society is much more than its buildings, amenities and infrastructure. It's the people around you who make the place feel like a community.

Have you made any special friendships or memories within the society?

I'll leave this one open for now. Not every memory needs to be documented, and not every friendship needs a formal mention. Some things are best left to unfold naturally!

What is one thing you would love to see happening more often in our community?

I'd love to see more active participation from the residents. A society becomes a better place when people move beyond being mere occupants and start taking an active interest in the community they are part of.

It shouldn't always be the same handful of people taking responsibility while everyone else watches from the sidelines. Even small contributions, when made collectively, can bring about meaningful improvements.

If you could introduce one new activity, event, or initiative in our society, what would it be?

I'd love to see society-wide sporting events, organised with the same enthusiasm and participation as our cultural celebrations.

We already have opportunities to come together through cultural activities. Adding sporting events could bring another dimension to community bonding. Table tennis, badminton, cricket, football or even some fun team competitions – there could be something for everyone, irrespective of age or skill level.

A little healthy competition, some team spirit and plenty of laughter could make these events something residents genuinely look forward to.

What message would you like to share with your fellow residents?

If you cannot actively bring about a positive change for the betterment of the society, at least support those who are putting in the effort to make it happen.

It's easy to criticise, point out shortcomings or expect others to take responsibility. But a community progresses only when its members contribute, cooperate and support constructive efforts. Not everyone has to lead, but everyone can play a part.

Part 6: The surprise question

What is one piece of advice you would give your younger self?

Start saving like your life depends on it!

It may sound like simple advice, but financial discipline is something you truly appreciate as you get older. Enjoy life, chase experiences and make memories, but don't forget to save along the way. Your future self will thank you for it!
