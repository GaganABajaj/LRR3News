Two Killed in Drone Attack on King Fahd International Airport

By Reuters  ·  22 Oct 2026 · 22:54

Saudi Arabia’s General Authority of Civil Aviation (GACA) confirmed that an attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam resulted in two fatalities – a Saudi national and an Indian resident – and wounded eight others. The incident occurred on Wednesday and, according to GACA, airport operations have since resumed with normal air traffic.

On the same day, Saudi air defences intercepted a drone targeting Saudi Aramco’s Shaybah oilfield, leading to two additional deaths, as reported by the Ministry of Energy.

The Houthi movement, also known as Ansar Allah, claimed responsibility for multiple attacks on Saudi infrastructure over the previous 48 hours, citing King Fahd International Airport, Abha International Airport, the Saudi defence ministry in Riyadh, and Aramco facilities in Yanbu as targets.

These attacks follow a series of recent assaults on Saudi airports, including a projectile strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on October 11 that killed one person and injured 11, and a prior attack on the same airport on October 10 that killed 12 and injured more than 300.

The Saudi‑led coalition reported intercepting and destroying four drones in the Riyadh and Medina regions. Reuters and AFP cited sources saying Saudi Aramco ordered staff evacuation from its Dhahran headquarters after a Houthi strike earlier in the month. The U.S. State Department also issued security alerts and began relocating diplomatic personnel from Saudi missions.
