R3 Solar Project: Need for Financial Clarity and Accountability

By Community Desk  ·  04 Nov 2026 · 12:05

The revised payment arrangement marks a change in the way the solar project will be funded. However, residents still deserve clarity on the overall project cost, the use of society reserves and the financial limits approved for its implementation.

Why this follow-up?

Life Republic R3 News has previously published articles discussing the importance of society bye-laws, the financial benefits of solar energy and the questions surrounding the proposed solar power project.

The objective has been to encourage informed discussion about a project that could potentially reduce the society's common electricity expenses.

Recent discussions and decisions provide an opportunity to examine how the project has progressed, particularly with regard to its funding arrangement, estimated cost and the financial authority given to the Managing Committee.

The intention is not to oppose the solar initiative. Rather, it is to ensure that a project involving substantial contributions from residents is implemented with transparency, proper documentation and financial discipline.

1. A change in the funding arrangement

During the 2025 meeting, a proposal was approved for installing a solar power system with an estimated financial commitment between ₹1-2 crore (exact amount can be checked in AGM 2025 MOM), excluding the possibility of additional expenditure.

The original funding arrangement included the use of available society reserves, advance maintenance collections and an additional monthly contribution from members to replenish the reserves.

However, during the latest meeting, a revised collection arrangement was proposed.

It was stated that members would contribute towards the project through two demand notices:

  • 75% of the individual contribution by 31 December 2026.
  • The remaining 25% by 31 March 2027.

It was further stated that project funds would come from member contributions and, wherever necessary, temporary utilisation of the society's designated reserve fund. Any amount utilised from this reserve would be replenished once the member contributions were received.

This represents a change from the earlier monthly recovery arrangement.

However, it is important to clarify whether the earlier monthly contribution arrangement has been formally withdrawn and how the advance maintenance collections approved previously have been accounted for.

A consolidated explanation would help residents understand the complete funding position and avoid any possibility of overlapping collections.

2. The biggest unanswered question: Where is the spending limit?

The most important issue is not simply how much each member is being asked to contribute. It is the total expenditure the society is authorised to incur.

The 2025 resolution approved a substantial project budget and authorised implementation within the approved financial limit.

The latest proposal refers to a project cost exceeding the earlier estimate, along with applicable taxes. However, a clearly defined revised maximum expenditure limit has not been specified.

This raises an important question:

Has the General Body approved a revised, all-inclusive project budget with a definite upper limit, or is the project proceeding without a clearly documented maximum expenditure?

A member contribution is not necessarily the same as an approved project budget. Similarly, permission to temporarily use society reserves does not automatically establish how much of those reserves can be utilised.

Residents should therefore receive clarification on:

  • What is the final project cost, including applicable taxes?
  • Does the revised member contribution represent the complete and final financial commitment?
  • What is the maximum amount of society reserves that may be used?
  • Has the earlier budget approval been formally revised?
  • If the project cost increases further, will fresh General Body approval be obtained before additional expenditure is committed?

A spending limit is not an obstacle to development. It is a basic financial safeguard that protects residents as well as the Managing Committee.

3. Is the use of society reserves supported by the registered bye-laws?

Society reserves are important financial resources intended to meet specific future requirements. Their utilisation must be consistent with the applicable provisions of the registered bye-laws and the law governing cooperative housing societies.

The Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act recognises the authority of the General Body while also requiring society funds to be used for permitted purposes.

The Government's Model Bye-laws describe certain specific purposes for which designated reserves may be utilised, including reconstruction, structural improvements and major repairs, subject to prescribed conditions.

A new solar power installation is not expressly apparent among these specific examples.

However, the registered bye-laws of Life Republic R3 must be examined before reaching any legal conclusion. Model Bye-laws should not automatically be treated as identical to the society's registered provisions.

The relevant question is:

Under which specific provision of R3's registered bye-laws is the designated reserve being utilised for the solar project?

The Managing Committee should share the applicable bye-law provision and the professional or legal justification supporting this decision.

The objective is not to suggest that the proposed utilisation is necessarily improper. It is to establish that the financial decision is supported by the appropriate legal authority.

4. Clarity on collections and financial records

The earlier meeting had also approved advance maintenance collections to support project cash flow and referred to maintaining an adequate operational reserve for regular society expenses.

During the latest discussions, it was stated that member contributions and additional use of society reserves would support the project. However, a complete reconciliation of the earlier financial arrangements would be useful.

Residents should receive clarification on:

  • Whether the advance maintenance collections were received and how they have been accounted for.
  • Whether any portion was utilised for the solar project.
  • The current balance of the designated reserve and the amount already utilised or earmarked.
  • The likely requirement for further temporary withdrawals.
  • The proposed timeline for restoring the reserve to its earlier position.

A separate project-wise financial statement would be a practical solution.

It could record member collections, available funds, withdrawals from designated reserves, vendor payments and subsequent replenishment.

It would also be useful to confirm that the revised demand arrangement replaces the earlier monthly recovery plan.

5. Are the projected savings realistic?

Solar energy can provide substantial long-term savings, but the actual financial benefits depend on several factors.

The earlier LRR3News solar calculator provided illustrative estimates based on assumed installation costs, electricity tariffs and daily power generation. It also indicated a relatively short payback period.

However, such calculations represent potential savings rather than guaranteed returns.

Actual savings depend on electricity consumption patterns, the proportion of generated electricity that offsets purchased power, applicable export credits, maintenance expenses, equipment degradation and changes in electricity tariffs.

The 2025 meeting discussions also referred to an estimated payback period of 36–48 months.

These projections should be supported by a detailed vendor assessment rather than relying solely on illustrative calculations.

Residents should be provided with:

  • Expected monthly and annual electricity generation.
  • Estimated reduction in common electricity bills.
  • Applicable electricity export and credit arrangements.
  • Annual maintenance and operating expenses.
  • Equipment warranty and replacement provisions.
  • Expected net savings and realistic payback calculations.

The financial assessment should be based on the final approved project cost.

6. Technical feasibility and existing solar installations

Another important consideration is whether the proposed installation has been properly assessed across all wings.

Residents have raised questions about available terrace space, existing infrastructure and the possibility of interference with solar water-heating installations.

These concerns should be addressed through a proper technical survey rather than assumptions.

The vendor should provide a wing-wise installation layout, structural assessment, shading analysis and details of waterproofing and maintenance access.

The society already has solar water-heating infrastructure. The proposed photovoltaic installation should not adversely affect its functioning or restrict access for routine maintenance.

A written technical assurance, supported by the vendor's assessment, would help address these concerns.

7. Has the subsidy eligibility been properly assessed?

The PM-Surya Ghar scheme provides for eligible residential group housing societies and RWAs to receive support for solar installations serving common facilities, subject to the applicable conditions.

The subsidy assessment may also require consideration of qualifying individual rooftop solar installations already installed by residents.

This is relevant because some residents may have installed individual solar systems on balconies or other permitted spaces.

Not every balcony installation automatically qualifies. Its eligibility depends on the scheme requirements, grid connectivity and applicable technical conditions.

The society should therefore clarify whether a survey of qualifying individual installations has been conducted and whether their capacity has been considered while assessing the available subsidy.

Residents should be able to examine the eligibility assessment, the proposed project capacity and the status of any subsidy application.

8. What should the Managing Committee clarify?

A consolidated disclosure would go a long way towards addressing residents' concerns.

The following information would be particularly useful:

  • Financial approval: The final project cost, including taxes, and a clearly defined maximum expenditure limit.
  • Vendor documentation: The detailed quotation, agreement, payment terms, warranties and performance commitments.
  • Funding arrangements: A complete statement of member collections, advance maintenance receipts and utilisation of society reserves.
  • Legal compliance: The applicable registered bye-law provisions supporting the proposed use of designated reserves.
  • Technical assessment: The installation layout, structural assessment and impact on existing solar water-heating systems.
  • Subsidy eligibility: The assessment of qualifying individual installations and the status of the subsidy application.
  • Financial projections: A realistic estimate of electricity generation, annual savings, maintenance expenses and payback period.

Members can also exercise their rights to inspect specified society records and obtain copies through the applicable procedure under the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act and the registered bye-laws.

Conclusion: A good initiative deserves equally good financial governance

The solar project has the potential to reduce common electricity expenses and provide long-term benefits to Life Republic R3.

It is a worthwhile initiative and deserves a fair assessment based on its technical and financial merits.

However, enthusiasm for renewable energy should go hand in hand with responsible financial management.

The revised collection arrangement explains how members are expected to contribute, but it does not fully answer the more important question of the maximum expenditure authorised for the project.

The use of society reserves, the treatment of earlier collections, the final project cost and the supporting technical and legal documentation also deserve greater clarity.

Publishing these details would not delay the project. Instead, it would strengthen confidence, reduce misunderstandings and protect the interests of residents and the society.

Transparency is not a sign of opposition. It is a sign of good governance.

The ultimate objective should be a technically sound, financially viable and transparently managed solar project that delivers measurable savings to Life Republic R3.
